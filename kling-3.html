Fighting Style

The first model to produce native 4K at 60fps — not upscaled, no AI-smoothing artifacts. Its brain is a multi-shot storyboard: up to six labeled shots in one generation, each with its own framing, motion and duration. The mindset shift from the official guide: “Don't think in prompts. Think in shots.”

✚Fighting for you

  • Native 4K@60fps — the only true broadcast/IMAX-grade output on this card. No upscale pass, no smoothing artifacts.
  • Multi-shot storyboard: a full 30s spot from one prompt — six shots with independent framing, motion and duration.
  • Photoreal human performance — strong bodies and faces (improved over 2.6), especially full-body and crowd scenes.
  • Native multi-character dialogue with the P1–P4 discipline (structured naming, visual anchoring, audio details, temporal control) + multi-dialect support.
  • The QFAI hero pipeline's engine: GPT Image 2 first frame → Kling i2v → final edit. Strongest i2v on the card for product films.
  • Cost-efficient workhorse: ~$0.50 per 10s @1080p, ~45–60s generation — built for iteration.

✖Fighting against you

  • The model forgets who it's looking at between shots — identity must be re-anchored at the start of every shot description.
  • Vague motion = averaged motion — “walks” becomes a generic shuffle; you must write the gait.
  • “Good lighting” means nothing to it — unspecified light produces inconsistent results by design.
  • No in-place editing — fixing one thing means re-rolling the scene (that's Omni's division).
  • Reference-image conditioning less precise than Sora 2 Pro cameos; Element images must all be 1:1 square or generation breaks.
  • Slower per generation at high resolution, and a thinner feature-flag ecosystem than Google's stack.
Corner verdict
Pick it for hero brand films at native 4K, multi-shot storyboard spots, and photoreal human performance. It's the default engine of the QFAI Cinematic Hero pipeline. The crowd is right — “for all of its faults, still the king of realistic performances.” Its faults are all promptable away: re-anchor identity per shot, specify lighting in four dimensions, write explicit motion verbs.

The Prompting Game Plan

From the vault's Kling 3.0 dossier (fal.ai official guide + QFAI production pipeline). Five moves, in order.

1

The structure — lean hard on explicit camera vocabulary

[Camera shot + movement] [Subject + identity] [Action]
[Environment + lighting + ambience] [Style/grade]
[Multi-shot storyboard breakdown if applicable]

Kling 3.0 is unusually responsive to camera grammar: orbit / 360° orbit (hero reveal), dolly-in/out, tracking shot, crane/jib up (scale reveal), whip-pan (beat transition), static/locked-off (beauty shot), Dutch tilt (tension).

2

Think in shots — the multi-shot storyboard (up to 6)

Each shot needs all four: label + duration · shot size + camera · subject anchor · one dominant action.

SHOT 1 — 3s — wide low-angle dolly-in
A lone runner crests a hill at dawn; sun behind her; warm rim.

SHOT 2 — 2s — extreme close-up static
Her water bottle's logo catches the light; condensation slides down.

SHOT 3 — 4s — medium tracking
She drinks; lowers the bottle; squints into the sun; smile.

The model forgets identity between shots. Anchor the character at the start of every shot: “SHOT 1 — ALEX (tall, short silver hair, navy jacket) — …” · “SHOT 3 — SAM (younger, hoodie, glasses) enters frame — two-shot with ALEX.” Never assume it remembers ALEX from SHOT 1.

3

Lighting in four dimensions — or get inconsistency

Official guidance: “good lighting” produces inconsistent results because it means nothing specific. Specify:

  • Quality — hard · soft · diffused · contrasty
  • Source — window light · practical lamp · fluorescent grid · neon signage · fire · moonlight
  • Direction — side key · backlit · top-light · Rembrandt · butterfly
  • Ratio — 1:1 flat · 1:4 dramatic · 1:8 chiaroscuro
4

Explicit motion verbs — vague = averaged

// ❌ Vague                    ✅ Explicit
"walks across the room"    →  "walks with purpose, slight forward lean, heels striking first"
"turns to look"            →  "turns sharply to the left, chin lifts, eyes narrow"
"runs"                     →  "sprints, full extension, arms pumping, body forward 30°"
"looks sad"                →  "jaw drops slightly, eyes gloss, head tilts down 10°"
5

Native dialogue — the P1–P4 principles

  • P1 Structured Naming: a consistent short name tag before every line. No “he said”, no “the tall one replies.”
  • P2 Visual Anchoring: describe the speaker's body position and gaze before the line — grounding prevents floating heads.
  • P3 Audio Details: delivery + ambient per shot. Descriptors: weary · hushed · sharp · excited · nervous · commanding · warm · cold · sardonic.
  • P4 Temporal Control: segment duration = line timing. <8 words → 2s · 8–15 words → 3s · two-line exchange → 4–5s · reaction beat → 1–2s.
SHOT 1 — 3s — medium two-shot — dim conference room, laptop glow
ALEX (50s, silver hair, grey suit jacket) sits at the head of the table.
SAM (30s, hoodie, glasses) sits across from him.
ALEX (delivery: measured, quiet): "The build is done."

SHOT 2 — 2s — close-up on SAM's face
SAM looks up slowly from his laptop.
SAM (delivery: disbelief, hushed): "Already?"
6

The 3-layer input system (QFAI hero pipeline)

The production pipeline runs GPT Image 2 → Kling 3.0 → final edit. Master the three layers or it fails:

  • Layer 1 — start_image_url (16:9): the cinematic first frame. A frozen still — describe what is, not what's about to happen.
  • Layer 2 — Elements (all 1:1 square): per-character identity lock. Each Element = frontal close-up + full-body/side references, numbered by upload order. Mixing aspect ratios breaks generation.
  • Layer 3 — multi-prompt segments: each segment calls @Element1, @Element2… at the start to re-anchor identity during that segment's motion.
7

Settings recipes

4K, 24fps — cinematic / TVC · 4K, 30fps — informational / explainer · 1080p, 60fps — kinetic sport spots.
