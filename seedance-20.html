Fighting Style

The fighter that takes text + image + video + audio in a single call and routes each material independently — identity anchors, motion references, style/VFX clips, beat tracks. Its signature move: cuts that land on the bass hit automatically. It has a successor (2.5), but it's not retired — it's the finisher.

✚Fighting for you

  • All-in-one reference entry point: 9 images + 3 video clips + 3 audio files in one generation — the prompt engine routes each material independently.
  • Beat-matched cuts are nearly automatic — its strongest native capability. Music-video commercials and montages live here.
  • 4K / 10-bit HEVC delivery — the resolution 2.5 gave up. Still the Seedance route for broadcast/CTV.
  • Camera-movement references: attach a clip showing the move; the model reproduces the path.
  • VFX / creative-template reproduction: attach an effect clip (glitch reveal, ink-wash dissolve) and it gets reproduced on your subject.
  • Cheap tiers: base $0.15/s, Mini $0.08/s — the volume fighter of the card.

✖Fighting against you

  • 15-second ceiling — longer stories mean stitching halves (or moving to 2.5).
  • Slow-mo physics collapse: struck balls reverse direction, worst in slow motion; object duplication on contact ~1-in-3 rolls.
  • Refuses environment transformations — a commanded park→stadium swap gets grounded back into a realistically-lit pitch (physical plausibility bias).
  • Reserved safe zones get filled — “upper third clean” is honored weakly; the model centers and enlarges the hero.
  • Gibberish on-pack text — pseudo-words invented on packaging despite explicit no-text instructions.
  • 60fps requests are silently ignored — every output is 24/1 fps. It's a confirmation, not an accident.
Corner verdict
Pick it for music-video commercials, beat-matched montages, and any deliverable that must land at 1080p or 4K. The house rule since Aug 2026: Seedance 2.5 for duration and editing, Seedance 2.0 for the final-resolution finish — a two-model pipeline at ~⅔ the cost of doing it all in 2.5.

The Prompting Game Plan

From the vault's Seedance 2.0 dossier + our 15-clip baseline sweep (verbatim prompts recovered from session transcripts). The framework is a decision lookup, not a template to fill.

1

The formula — length scales with complexity

[Subject] + [Action] + [Environment] + [Camera Movement] + [Mood/Style]

Single-shot scene  → 2–3 sentences.
Multi-shot cinematic → 200–500+ words with explicit shot-by-shot timing.
2

@[Material Name] — the reference anchor syntax

Label each uploaded file with a bracketed material name, then call it in the prompt. Names are case-sensitive and must match the uploaded label exactly. Use @[Name] every time you reference that asset — not just the first mention.

@[Character] A woman in her 30s with short red hair and a navy blazer.
@[Product] A matte black thermos with a silver ring and embossed logo.
@[Location] Rooftop terrace at golden hour, city skyline blurred behind.
@[BeatTrack] The uploaded EDM track — cuts must align to bass hits.

@[Character] enters the rooftop. She picks up @[Product], holds it toward
the skyline. Dolly push-in, shallow DOF. Cuts synced to @[BeatTrack].

Images → identity anchors · Video clips → motion / style / temporal anchors · Audio → timing anchors.

3

The capability catalog — use it as a decision lookup

  • Consistency: attach 2–4 @[Character] images from different angles → identity locks across the full clip.
  • Camera movement reference: “Orbit @[Hero] using the @[CameraRef] camera path. Slow 360°, 6 seconds.”
  • VFX reproduction: “Reproduce the @[StyleClip] transition to reveal @[Product]. 2 seconds.”
  • Extension: attach @[BaseClip], prompt for what happens next from its last frame.
  • Beat-matched cuts: “Cut to @[Product] wide shot at the first bass drop (@[Track] 00:04). Cut to ECU at the second drop (00:08).”
  • Emotional performance / lip-sync: attach a voice performance as @[Voice]; it generates lip-sync and facial emotion from the audio.
4

Prompt around the known-weak regimes (our A/B baseline)

We documented exactly where 2.0 breaks, on identical prompts — and what hedges work:

  • Struck-ball physics: add explicit trajectory language + “the ball travels one direction and exits frame” (slow motion is the collapse zone — if the shot must be slow-mo, budget extra rolls).
  • Object duplication on contact: name the object count and state (“one ball, at foot until impact, then in flight”) to hedge the 1-in-3 spawn rate.
  • Environment transformation: describe the transformed environment as already real (lighting, crowd, signage) — plausibility bias will ground anything that reads “magical.”
  • Reserved zones: don't promise them. Comp the end card over whatever survives, or generate a clean plate.
  • Packaging text: assume gibberish — composite the label in post unless the test render proves otherwise.
5

Settings that actually exist

Resolution: 480p / 720p / 1080p / 4K (10-bit HEVC) · Duration ≤15s · Aspect per task (i2v inherits the reference's ratio — build refs at the ratio you want). Frame rate is not a parameter: expect 24/1 on every output, and if ffprobe says 30 or 60, post-gen Interpolation was left on and that clip's motion score is void.
